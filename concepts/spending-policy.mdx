Giving an AI agent access to a real wallet means you need real guardrails — not just settings in a config file that a clever prompt could theoretically route around. Nebula enforces your spending policy in two places: in the Hub's policy engine (which runs before every transaction is signed) and optionally in a **Soroban smart contract** on the Stellar blockchain, which provides an independent second layer of enforcement that the Hub itself cannot bypass.

This page explains every check in the policy matrix, what each outcome means, and how to configure your policy from the dashboard.

## The confirmation matrix

Every transfer is evaluated through a sequence of checks in order. The first check that matches determines the outcome — subsequent checks are not evaluated.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Paused">
    If your policy is set to **paused**, every transaction is rejected immediately, regardless of destination, amount, or any other field.

    **Outcome: `reject` with reason `policy_paused`**

    Use this when you want to temporarily freeze all agent spending — for example, while you investigate unusual activity or reconfigure your policy.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Denylist">
    If the destination address appears in your **denylist**, the transaction is rejected.

    **Outcome: `reject` with reason `destination_denylisted`**

    Add addresses to the denylist to permanently block your agent from ever sending funds to them, regardless of amount.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Daily cap">
    The Hub tracks total USDC-equivalent spend in the last 24 hours. If adding the current transfer would exceed your **daily cap**, the transaction is rejected.

    **Outcome: `reject` with reason `exceeds_daily_cap`**

    The daily cap resets on a rolling 24-hour window, not at midnight. XLM transfers are converted to USDC using the live XLM/USD rate before this check runs.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Per-transaction cap">
    If the single transfer amount (in USDC equivalent) exceeds your **per-transaction cap**, it is rejected.

    **Outcome: `reject` with reason `exceeds_per_tx_cap`**

    The whitelist never bypasses the per-tx cap. Even a known, trusted address cannot receive more than this limit in a single transaction.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Micro threshold">
    If the transfer amount is at or below your **micro threshold** (and it has not already been rejected above), it is auto-approved without requiring any human confirmation.

    **Outcome: `auto` with reason `within_micro_threshold`**

    The micro threshold is your friction-free zone. Set it to the maximum amount you are comfortable with your agent spending without asking — for example, $1 USDC for small API payments.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Whitelist">
    If the destination address is in your **whitelist** and the amount is within the per-tx cap (and above the micro threshold), the transaction is auto-approved.

    **Outcome: `auto` with reason `whitelisted_within_per_tx_cap`**

    Add frequently-used, trusted addresses (for example, your own wallets or known service providers) to the whitelist so your agent can pay them without prompting you each time.
  </Step>

  <Step title="New destination — confirmation required">
    If the destination is not on the denylist, the amount is within the daily and per-tx caps, the amount is above the micro threshold, and the destination is not on the whitelist — the transaction requires **human confirmation**.

    **Outcome: `confirm` with reason `new_destination_requires_confirmation`**

    Your agent receives a `confirmation_required` response with a `confirmation_id`. It will wait for you to approve or reject from the dashboard.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The three outcomes

| Outcome | What happens |
|---|---|
| `auto` | The Hub signs the transaction immediately and broadcasts it to Stellar. The tool returns `status: "ok"` with the transaction hash. |
| `confirm` | The Hub creates a pending approval record and returns `status: "confirmation_required"`. The agent must poll with `await_confirmation` while you review the request in the dashboard. |
| `reject` | No transaction is created. The tool returns `status: "rejected"` with a reason string. The agent should surface this to you as an error. |

## USDC amounts and XLM conversion

All policy caps are denominated in **USDC**. When your agent calls `transfer` with an `amount_xlm` value, the Hub fetches the live XLM/USD exchange rate and converts the amount to USDC before running the policy matrix.

This means your caps work consistently regardless of whether your agent is sending XLM or USDC directly — you always set limits in a stable, dollar-denominated unit.

## Configuring your policy

All policy settings are managed from the **Policy** page in your dashboard at [nebulaonchain.xyz](https://nebulaonchain.xyz). You can:

- Set per-transaction and daily caps (in USDC).
- Set the micro threshold (in USDC).
- Add and remove addresses from the whitelist and denylist.
- Pause and unpause the policy instantly.

Changes take effect immediately for all subsequent tool calls — there is no deployment step.

<Note>
  When the `POLICY_CONTRACT_ID` environment variable is configured on the Hub, policy caps are also enforced by a Soroban smart contract on Stellar. On-chain enforcement acts as a second, independent check that does not rely on Hub database state — even if the Hub were somehow bypassed, the contract would reject out-of-bounds transactions.
</Note>

## Recommended starting configuration

<Tip>
  A good starting point: set the micro threshold to $1 USDC (covers most small API payments without interruption), the per-tx cap to $10 USDC, and the daily cap to $50 USDC. Whitelist addresses you pay regularly. Raise limits only as you gain confidence in your agent's behaviour.
</Tip>
